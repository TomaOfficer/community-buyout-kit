Community buyout brief · November 12, 2026

99 Union Square

Could Milford neighbors buy this building to keep its storefronts available to local businesses? First, find out whether the owner is still considering offers.

The building

The broker’s brochure describes two ground-floor storefronts and an upstairs apartment on the Milford Oval. It puts the building at about 3,226 square feet. LoopNet shows 2,593 square feet and says it has stopped advertising the property.

The brochure advertised a $600,000 asking price, and an older snapshot of LoopNet’s search results still showed it for sale. We still need the owner’s current position and an explanation of how the floor areas were measured.

What if?

A neighborhood ownership group could buy and manage the building, with local businesses continuing to operate the storefronts. Start with the current leases and ask tenants what they need to stay. The group would need rents that work for tenants and cover the cost of owning the building.

The money

At the old advertised price of $600,000, an illustrative 25% down payment would be $150,000, leaving $450,000 to borrow. This example excludes closing costs, repairs, and reserves. Current price, rents, operating costs, and loan terms are unknown.

Fundraising

Ask a lender what the building’s documented income could support. Then work out how much the group would need to raise and whether neighbors would contribute. A funding target depends on the price, expenses, and financing terms.

Ownership and local rules

Ask a New Hampshire real estate lawyer how a neighborhood group could hold title, make decisions, and take over the leases. If the plan would change how any space is used, ask Milford’s building or planning office what approvals would apply.

Next steps

  1. Ask the broker whether the owner is still considering offers and what price and timeline they have in mind.
  2. If the property is available, request current leases, the rent roll, recent operating statements, and a measured floor plan.
  3. Talk with neighbors about what they want to preserve and who can help review the numbers.
