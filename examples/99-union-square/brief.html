What happens next

  1. Confirm availability. Ask the broker if the owner is considering offers and what price and timeline they have in mind.
  2. Ask for the records. If it’s available, request current leases, the rent roll, recent operating statements, and a measured floor plan.
  3. Talk with neighbors. Ask what they want to preserve and who can help review the numbers.

What community ownership could do

A neighborhood group could buy and manage the building while local businesses continue to run the storefronts. The group would need rents that work for tenants and cover the cost of owning the property.

The building

The broker’s brochure describes two ground-floor storefronts and one apartment upstairs, with about 3,226 square feet. LoopNet’s property page reports 2,593 square feet and says it has stopped advertising the property.

An older LoopNet search snapshot still showed the building for sale. The owner’s plans, current tenancy, and how each source measured the building remain unconfirmed.

Fundraising

Once the group has the rent roll and operating statements, ask a lender how much debt the building’s income could support. That gives neighbors a basis for discussing what they might need to raise together.

Ownership and local rules

Ask a New Hampshire real estate lawyer how the group could hold title, make decisions, and take over the leases. If the group proposes a change of use, ask Milford’s building or planning office which approvals would apply.
